Know your rights

Denied before? Ask again.

Black veterans have long faced barriers to benefits they earned. After World War II, many were shut out of GI Bill home loans and college benefits by local, segregated administration. More recently, records obtained through litigation by advocates showed Black veterans were denied disability compensation at higher rates than white veterans.

A denial is not the final word. You have the right to appeal, the right to free representation, and the right to see your file.

See claims & appeals help

  1. Get your records

    Request your C-file from VA and your service records (DD-214) from the National Archives.

  2. Get a free, accredited advocate

    Veterans Service Organizations and county veterans offices file claims at no cost. Never pay to file an initial claim.

  3. Choose your review path

    Supplemental claim, higher-level review, or Board appeal. Each has deadlines, so act within a year of your decision.

  4. Fix the discharge, too

    An other-than-honorable discharge can block benefits. Discharge review boards can upgrade it, especially where PTSD, TBI, or bias played a role.

Heritage

Generations of courage

Black service members defended freedom abroad while fighting for it at home. Know the legacy you carry.

  1. 1866

    Buffalo Soldiers

    Congress creates all-Black regiments in the peacetime Army. They serve on the frontier, in Cuba, the Philippines, and beyond.

  2. 1918

    Harlem Hellfighters

    The 369th Infantry spends 191 days in combat in World War I, longer than any other American unit of its size.

  3. 1941

    Tuskegee Airmen

    The first Black military pilots train in Alabama and go on to fly with distinction over Europe and North Africa.

  4. 1942

    Montford Point Marines

    The first Black Marines train at segregated Montford Point, North Carolina. About 20,000 pass through before 1949.

  5. 1945

    The Six Triple Eight

    855 Black women of the 6888th Central Postal Directory Battalion clear a backlog of 17 million pieces of mail in Europe.

  6. 1948

    Executive Order 9981

    President Truman orders equal treatment in the armed forces, opening the door to full integration.

  7. Today

    Your story

    From Korea and Vietnam to the Gulf, Iraq, and Afghanistan, the line continues. It runs through you.

FAQ

Straight answers

Do I have to pay someone to help with my VA claim?

No. Accredited Veterans Service Organizations, county veterans service officers, and many legal clinics help for free. Be wary of anyone charging to file an initial claim.

My claim was denied years ago. Is it too late?

Usually not. You can file a supplemental claim with new and relevant evidence at any time. New laws such as the PACT Act have also expanded eligibility for many conditions.

Can I get VA care with an other-than-honorable discharge?

Often, yes. VA can provide mental health care in many cases, and you can apply for a character-of-discharge review or a discharge upgrade. Free legal help is listed in the directory.

Are there benefits for my family?

Yes. Survivors and dependents may qualify for compensation, education benefits, health coverage, and burial benefits. Caregivers can get training and support too.

Is this site run by the VA?

No. This is an independent community resource. It links to official VA programs and trusted organizations, but it does not process claims or collect personal information.
